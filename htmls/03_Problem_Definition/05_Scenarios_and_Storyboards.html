Scenarios and Storyboards

Purpose

This document shows how Canopy Pause supports the main user situations identified through discovery research. The scenarios focus on the final V6 concept and demonstrate the complete pause-reset-return loop.

Scenario A: Deep Focus Solo Study

Situation

A student is studying alone in the CDU library. They are reading, coding, debugging, or writing notes and have been focused for a long period. They know they need a break, but stopping suddenly feels risky because they may lose the task thread.

What happens Design response
The student is deep in work and begins to feel tired. Focus Session offers a gentle reset option without forcing an immediate break.
The student wants to stop only after finishing the current step. The app asks, "Pause after your current task?" and allows delay.
The student writes what they will finish before leaving. Study Handoff captures a stopping point.
The student writes what they will resume after returning. Study Handoff captures the return task anchor.
The student takes a short reset. Timer keeps the break bounded and shows a brief nature prompt.
The student returns to the library. Resume Focus shows "Start here" with the saved task.

Scenario B: Comfort-Aware Nature Reset

Situation

A student wants a proper reset away from the screen, but outdoor breaks feel uncertain in Darwin. They need to know whether a nearby place is shaded, seated, quiet enough, and worth the walking time. If outdoor conditions are poor, they still need a valid alternative.

What happens Design response
The student reaches a natural pause point. The app offers "Use my usual reset" as the fastest path.
The student checks whether the suggested spot is worth leaving for. Recommendation shows total time, shade, seating likelihood, breeze, and crowd level.
The student wants to understand the recommendation. "Why this spot?" explains that comfort data is a prototype estimate.
Outdoor conditions are acceptable. Reset Journey gives simple landmark directions instead of a complex map.
The student arrives and starts the timer. Nature Reset Timer shows one short prompt, such as noticing breeze or shade.
If outdoor conditions are poor, the student chooses an indoor reset instead. Indoor Nature Reset offers a window or indoor nature-view option without framing it as a failed break.

Scenario C: Group Study Reset

Situation

A small group is studying together. The group loses focus and wants a short break, but group breaks often turn into long conversations. No one wants to be the person who tells everyone to return.

What happens Design response
The group notices that study momentum has dropped. Focus Session includes Group Reset as a secondary action.
The group chooses size and break length. Group Setup captures group size and 5, 7, or 10 minute break length.
The group saves what they will resume. Group handoff records the shared return task.
The app recommends a suitable spot. Group Recommendation shows shade, crowd level, and capacity wording.
The group starts a shared timer. Share options include QR code, link, or start on one phone.
The timer ends. Group Return Cue prompts everyone to head back and shows the shared task anchor.

Final Scenario Summary

The scenarios show that Canopy Pause is strongest when it supports the complete study-break cycle. It does not only point students toward outdoor spaces. It helps them stop safely, choose a comfortable nature-connected reset, keep the break short, and return to study with a clear next task.
