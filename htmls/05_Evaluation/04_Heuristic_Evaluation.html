Heuristic Evaluation: Final Prototype V6

Detailed Heuristic Notes

1. Visibility of System Status

Issue: Users may not know whether comfort information is real, live, simulated, or estimated.

Final response: The interface uses plain, transparent wording:

  • Prototype estimate
  • Likely free
  • Checked recently
  • Recent spot feedback

Reason: This gives enough confidence for Wizard-of-Oz testing without overclaiming live sensor accuracy.

2. Match Between System and Real World

Issue: Numeric comfort scores felt abstract and did not match how students naturally judge a break spot.

Final response: The prototype uses student-friendly comfort tags:

  • strong shade,
  • light breeze,
  • medium crowd,
  • indoor seating,
  • no seating,
  • quick standing reset,
  • seats 2, space for 4 standing.

Reason: These labels are easier to understand than a score such as 82/100.

3. User Control and Freedom

Issue: Students rejected random or pushy break prompts.

Final response: The final prototype gives control through:

  • Pause after your current task?
  • Use my usual reset
  • Choose reset style
  • Indoor Nature Reset
  • Skip today
  • Change anytime

Reason: The app supports breaks without taking control away from the student.

4. Consistency and Standards

Issue: Earlier prototypes used multiple terms for similar ideas.

Final response: V6 standardises key labels:

Concept Final label
remembered preference Usual reset
fallback indoors Indoor Nature Reset
return instruction Start here
estimated comfort data Prototype estimate
shared group break Group reset

5. Error Prevention

Issue: The app should not push outdoor breaks during heat, rain, poor shade, or poor seating.

Final response: V6 provides Indoor Nature Reset and clear reasons for staying inside, such as:

Better option right now: heat is high outside.

Reason: Comfort before persuasion is central to the design.

6. Recognition Rather Than Recall

Issue: Students often lose the study thread after a break.

Final response: The app repeatedly shows the saved return anchor:

  • on the nature timer,
  • on the return cue,
  • on the resume screen.

7. Flexibility and Efficiency of Use

Issue: Prototype 4 added useful preferences but required users to choose each time.

Final response: V6 makes the fastest path:

Use my usual reset → Study handoff → Start reset → Return → Resume

Style changing remains available but secondary.

8. Help Users Recognise and Recover

Issue: Breaks may drift longer than intended.

Final response: The timer and return cue are clear. Extension is still allowed but not emphasised:

Need 2 more minutes?

Reason: The main action should encourage return, not extension.

9. Privacy and Data Transparency

Issue: Location and simulated sensor data could feel like tracking.

Final response: V6 avoids background-tracking language and uses:

  • start point,
  • based on last reset,
  • prototype estimate,
  • change anytime.

10. Human-Nature Fit

Issue: Nature should not become a forced productivity trick.

Final response: Nature prompts are brief and grounded:

  • Notice the breeze moving through the trees.
  • Look outside and notice one tree, cloud, or patch of shade.
  • Notice the shade and breeze around the courtyard.
