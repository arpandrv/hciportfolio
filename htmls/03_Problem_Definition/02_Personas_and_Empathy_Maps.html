Personas and Empathy Maps

These are combined personas, not direct copies of one participant. Each persona combines similar behaviours, needs, and barriers from multiple students.

Persona 1: The Flow-Protecting Solo Student

This student studies alone for long blocks, usually with reading-heavy, theory-heavy, coding, debugging, or documentation-based work. They avoid breaks because stopping feels risky. They worry that if they leave at the wrong time, they will lose the argument, the task structure, or the mental model they were holding in their head.

Says Worried About Does
"I'll say okay after this paragraph." If I leave now, I might lose the thread. Keeps reading, coding, or debugging even after focus drops.
"The feeling that it's not the right moment." Stopping now might cost more time than continuing. Drinks water or looks away from the screen instead of leaving.
"Writing down what I was about to do next." I need something concrete to restart from. Leaves notes like page number, next section, function to check, or next question.

Needs

  • A gentle prompt that can be snoozed until the current section, task, or step is finished.
  • A one-line task anchor before leaving.
  • A return screen that immediately shows where to restart.
  • A very short nearby break option, preferably one to two minutes away.

Persona 2: The Low-Effort Phone-Break Student

This student often studies through practice questions, slides, accounting problems, or short task sets. They usually know when they are getting tired, but their default break is the easiest one: coffee, phone scrolling, a casual phone game, stretching near the desk, or walking around inside. Outdoor breaks sound useful, but they feel like too much effort unless the app removes almost all decision-making.

Says Thinks Does
"I always think it'll be short. Like I tell myself five minutes." Going outside is more effort than just staying here. Opens Instagram, messages, or a phone game.
"One round only." This is easier than finding somewhere outside. Keeps playing or scrolling longer than planned.
"show me one good option. Not ten options." If I have to choose too much, I'll close the app. Stays near the desk or inside the library.

Needs

  • One clear nearby outdoor option, not many choices.
  • Clear walking time and total break time.
  • Confirmed shade and seating.
  • A low-distraction break activity that can replace phone scrolling or games.
  • A simple return prompt.

Persona 3: The Outdoor Reset Seeker

This student is open to outdoor breaks and may already believe that stepping outside helps. Their main issue is uncertainty. They do not always know which spots are shaded, quiet, seated, close, or worth walking to. For them, a bad outdoor break is worse than staying inside because heat, crowding, and noise make the break feel wasted.

Says Thinks Does
"there is a shaded bench two minutes from the library door," I would probably go. Will it actually be shaded and quiet? Stays inside or stretches near the desk when unsure.
"I don't feel like I've had a real break." A nearby spot is not useful if it is noisy or uncomfortable. Looks for quieter shaded areas.
"One sentence is fine. I can handle one sentence." I want to notice the environment, not do a whole meditation. Notices breeze, trees, shade, or quietness when the spot works.

Needs

  • A reliable map or route to nearby shaded spaces.
  • Spot information such as shade, seating, quietness, crowd level, distance, and breeze/heat.
  • Simple landmarks, not just map pins.
  • A clear end time so the break does not become open-ended.
  • Short nature-noticing prompts.

Persona 4: The Group Study Student

This student studies with friends, often for IT certification or practice-based work. Group study makes it easier to decide to take a break, but harder to return on time. The break becomes social, and no one wants to be the person who ends the conversation. Outdoor breaks also need enough space for multiple people, not just one person.

Says Thinks Does
"The group just stops being productive." We need a break, but it probably won't stay short. Goes outside or near the entrance with friends.
"nobody has to be the annoying person who ends the break." I don't want to be the annoying one ending the fun. Keeps talking even after the planned break time.
"is there enough space for three, four people?" A bench for one person is not useful for us. Chooses places near the entrance if group seating is uncertain.

Needs

  • A shared group break timer.
  • A group-capacity tag showing whether a spot works for two, three, or four people.
  • Confirmed shade and seating/standing space.
  • A return reminder that alerts everyone at the same time.
  • A clear next task for the group after returning.

Summary of Persona Needs

Persona Main problem Most important support
Flow-Protecting Solo Student Fear of losing the study thread Task anchor and flow-sensitive break timing
Low-Effort Phone-Break Student Defaults to phone or indoor breaks because they are easier One-tap nearby outdoor break with timer
Outdoor Reset Seeker Wants outdoor breaks but needs comfort certainty Shade, seating, quietness, distance, landmark information and short nature-noticing prompts
Group Study Student Breaks extend because of conversation and group dynamics Shared timer and group-suitable spot information
