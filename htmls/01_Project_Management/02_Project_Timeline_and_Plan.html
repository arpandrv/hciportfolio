Project Timeline and Plan

This project plan describes the main steps completed by the team. It is organised by project stage rather than by strict weekly dates because the work developed iteratively across the semester.

Step 1: Form the Team and Agree on Project Direction

The team formed Group 24 and discussed possible directions for the HIT381 human-nature interaction project. The team selected a problem area connected to CDU students, long study sessions, campus outdoor spaces, and short restorative breaks.

Main activities:

  • Formed the project team.
  • Reviewed the human-nature interaction theme.
  • Discussed possible user groups and contexts.
  • Chose CDU library students as the target user group.
  • Narrowed the project direction toward study breaks, nearby nature, shade, heat, and study momentum.

Main outputs:

  • Initial project direction.
  • Target user group.
  • Early design problem area.

Step 2: Prepare Research and Ethics Materials

The team prepared the research plan and ethics materials before collecting participant data. This included defining the study aims, participant criteria, methods, consent process, and possible risks.

Main activities:

  • Prepared the ethics application.
  • Defined participant selection criteria.
  • Planned semi-structured interviews, observations, and prototype feedback.
  • Prepared consent procedures.
  • Considered risks such as time inconvenience, fatigue discussion, and outdoor heat.

Main outputs:

  • Ethics application.
  • Ethics clearance.
  • Consent forms.
  • Research procedure for interviews, observations, and prototype feedback.

Step 3: Recruit Participants and Collect Consent

The team recruited CDU student participants who regularly study in the library or similar study spaces. Consent was collected before participation.

Main activities:

  • Recruited CDU students aged 18 or older.
  • Explained the project purpose and participation requirements.
  • Collected consent before interviews and observations.
  • Used participant IDs to organise research data.

Main outputs:

  • Participant list.
  • Consent records.
  • Participant profile information.

Step 4: Conduct Discovery Research

The team conducted discovery research to understand how students study, how they notice fatigue, what breaks they take, and what prevents them from using outdoor campus spaces.

Main activities:

  • Conducted semi-structured interviews.
  • Collected observation notes about study and break behaviour.
  • Recorded participant patterns related to break timing, phone use, outdoor comfort, and return-to-study issues.
  • Organised transcripts and notes for analysis.

Main outputs:

  • Interview transcripts.
  • Observation notes.
  • Participant profiles.
  • Initial answers to research questions.

Step 5: Analyse Findings and Define User Needs

The team analysed the discovery data to identify repeated patterns and user needs. This stage helped move the project from general interest in outdoor breaks to a clearer design problem.

Main activities:

  • Reviewed interview transcripts and observation notes.
  • Identified recurring themes.
  • Compared participant barriers and motivations.
  • Developed personas and empathy maps.
  • Defined key user needs and design requirements.

Main outputs:

  • Thematic analysis.
  • Personas and empathy maps.
  • Research-question summary.
  • Problem statement.
  • Design requirements.

Step 6: Frame the Design Problem

The team refined the design problem based on the research findings. The final framing focused on helping students take short nature-connected reset breaks without losing study momentum.

Main activities:

  • Converted user needs into design requirements.
  • Identified the central problem of preserving the study thread.
  • Defined requirements for shade, seating, time, comfort, privacy, and return support.
  • Created user stories and scenarios.

Main outputs:

  • Trimmed problem statement and design requirements.
  • User stories.
  • Scenarios and storyboards.
  • Clear final design direction.

Step 7: Generate and Select Design Concepts

The team explored multiple design ideas and compared them against the research findings. The final concept combined the strongest parts of several ideas.

Main activities:

  • Generated early concepts such as map-based reset support, task-anchor support, one-tap reset, nature prompts, break timer, group timer, and indoor fallback.
  • Compared strengths and weaknesses of each idea.
  • Selected Canopy Pause as the final concept direction.
  • Defined the core pause-reset-return flow.

Main outputs:

  • Ideation and concept selection artefact.
  • Selected concept: Canopy Pause.
  • Final concept flow.

Step 8: Develop Prototype Iterations

The team developed the prototype through multiple iterations. Each iteration responded to user needs, feedback, and design problems found in the previous version.

Main activities:

  • Developed early map-first prototype ideas.
  • Shifted toward a study-flow-first design.
  • Added one-tap smart reset recommendation.
  • Added reset style options such as Closest, Quietest, Coolest, and Indoor Nature.
  • Added group reset support.
  • Finalised the V6 study-safe nature reset flow.

Main outputs:

  • Design iteration documentation.
  • Prototype iteration link.
  • Final V6 prototype direction.

Step 9: Plan and Conduct Evaluation

The team evaluated the final prototype using a mixed evaluation approach. The evaluation focused on usability, learnability, trust, nature fit, and return-to-study support.

Main activities:

  • Prepared the evaluation plan.
  • Created the Wizard-of-Oz protocol for simulated comfort data.
  • Conducted cognitive walkthrough.
  • Conducted heuristic evaluation.
  • Collected prototype review feedback.
  • Identified final design implications.

Main outputs:

  • Evaluation plan.
  • Wizard-of-Oz protocol.
  • Cognitive walkthrough.
  • Heuristic evaluation.
  • Prototype review responses.
  • Final concept specification.

Step 10: Refine Portfolio Artefacts

The team organised the portfolio so that it could be reviewed independently from the report. Artefacts were grouped into project management, discovery, problem definition, design, and evaluation sections.

Main activities:

  • Organised discovery research artefacts.
  • Joined interview transcripts into a single file.
  • Moved design and evaluation files into clearer folders.

Main outputs:

  • Structured portfolio folders.
  • Project management documents.
  • Joined interview transcript file.

Step 11: Prepare Final Report and Presentation

After completing the portfolio and evaluation artefacts, the team prepared the final report and presentation. The report synthesises and interprets the portfolio evidence, while the presentation communicates the final design concept, user needs, core tasks, and prototype value.

Main activities:

  • Prepare final report sections.
  • Link report claims back to portfolio evidence.
  • Prepare final presentation slides.
  • Prepare the product demonstration video.
  • Practise explaining the problem, users, design requirements, prototype, evaluation findings, limitations, and trade-offs.
  • Prepare for presentation questions and answers.

Main outputs:

  • Final project report.
  • Final presentation.
  • Product demonstration video.
  • Q&A preparation.
